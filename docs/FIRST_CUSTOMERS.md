# NeatQuote: first customers in Türkiye

NeatQuote helps independent home-cleaning businesses prepare consistent quotes, see their estimated profit, and follow up with customers. Start with a few owners who currently price jobs in messages or spreadsheets. Demand and willingness to pay have not been established; this plan is an experiment, not a revenue forecast.

## Offer to test

- Free: 3 quotes per calendar month.
- Paid experiment: **₺399 for 30 days of Pro access**, paid upfront. Access does not renew or charge automatically; the owner purchases another period to continue.
- Quote currency starts in TRY and can be changed. The NeatQuote access price is separate from the price a cleaning business quotes its own customer.

Ask owners what they use now and what they spend time on before explaining the product. Test whether the current price is worthwhile after they have prepared a real quote; do not treat a polite expression of interest as a purchase.

## Week 1: find a specific problem

1. Choose one city or district the founder knows. Build a list of **20 independent home cleaners or small cleaning firms** through manual public searches, local business listings, and permitted marketplace listings. Record the public business contact, business size if known, current quoting method if stated, and why the business seems relevant. Review marketplace and group rules before using their messaging channels; do not scrape private member information.
2. Request **5 short discovery conversations**. This is a target, not an existing result. Ask about the last cleaning job they priced: how they estimated time, what details they needed, what they sent the customer, and what happened afterward.
3. Offer to help enter their own service rates and branding, then prepare **one real cleaning-job quote** together. Let the owner decide which customer information to use. If there is no current job, use a clearly labeled practice quote and record it separately from real activation.
4. Write down where the workflow helped and where it caused friction. Prioritize repeated problems affecting the first quote over adding unrelated features.

Personalize the opening using a public business detail. Send messages manually through a suitable business contact or an allowed group channel. Respect a decline and avoid repeated follow-ups. No outreach has been sent as part of building this app.

## Week 2: test continued use and the paid offer

Check whether each participating owner prepared another quote without help, whether a customer accepted a quote, and what they still use outside the app. Ask the owner to confirm acceptance; an app status alone does not prove that a customer agreed or that a job was paid.

Present the ₺399 / 30-day offer only after useful hands-on experience. Explain that payment buys NeatQuote access, not cleaning services, and that it is prepaid with no automatic renewal. If live merchant checkout is not approved and configured, record purchase interest separately; do not count it as paid revenue or direct people to a placeholder checkout.

With permission, turn a real customer's account of using the app into a short case study. Show their actual experience and only use names, logos, and numbers they approve. There are no verified testimonials or conversion results yet.

## Review the funnel weekly

Keep a small spreadsheet using counts for each week. Do not put customer addresses or other unnecessary personal details in this launch tracker.

| Stage | Count when | What the number helps decide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevant businesses identified | A business fits the target and has a permitted public contact route | Whether the chosen niche is reachable |
| Discovery conversations | An owner actually discusses their current workflow | Whether the message starts useful conversations |
| Activated: first real quote | An owner creates a quote for an actual cleaning job | Whether onboarding delivers practical value |
| Customer accepted a quote | The cleaner confirms their first customer accepted an offer made in NeatQuote | Whether quotes are being used in real work |
| Paid NeatQuote customer | An approved, configured iyzico checkout confirms a successful payment | Whether users will pay the tested price |
| Repeat use | An owner creates another real quote in a later week | Whether the app is useful beyond the first demo |

Count each business once per funnel stage. For the same acquisition cohort, follow conversations → activated first quote → customer accepted → paid, recording when each event happens. Calculate conversion using businesses from the previous stage that reach the next stage, and keep the raw counts beside it; payment may happen before a cleaning customer accepts a quote, so keep those event dates rather than forcing an order. Track repeat use separately. For small samples, use the conversations to explain the numbers rather than treating percentages as reliable market estimates. Record payment interest, failed checkout, refunds, and successful payment separately.

If conversations happen but owners do not create real quotes, improve onboarding or revisit the problem. If owners use quotes but do not buy, ask what prevents payment and test one change to price or scope at a time. Continue the experiment rather than claiming the first few users establish a sustainable business.

## Before taking live payments

The owner must review the local TRY offer and confirm merchant approval, the receiving account, applicable tax and invoicing treatment, and the product's terms, privacy notice, cancellation, and refund conditions. These require the owner's actual business details and review; generic text is not a substitute.

The intended payment route is iyzico's hosted Checkout Form. Its official Node SDK includes TRY examples: <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/iyzico/iyzipay-node/master/README.md>. SDK support does not establish merchant eligibility, approval, or a live payout account. Use sandbox testing before enabling the live offer, and count revenue only after a real payment is confirmed.

This document is a customer-development plan. It does not establish that the app is publicly deployed, that live payments are enabled, or that any revenue has been earned.
